# Shorter candidates fix RAKE, not the 50% target

The "shorter keyphrase candidates" improvement is worth building, but it has to change how candidates are generated, not just cap their length. It should not be expected to reach AC-08.3's 50% bar. rake-nltk's `max_length` drops longer runs instead of splitting them, and RAKE adds up word scores, so a 4-word run always beats its own 2-word parts. That explains why 0 of 72 gold terms reached the top 10 when 71 of the 72 terms are 1 or 2 words long. Within the contract, the strongest option is project code with no new dependency: list every 1–2-word sub-n-gram over the existing technical tokenizer, rank with a score that gives no bonus for length, fold plurals, drop the bare technology name, and output the most frequent surface form. The second option is spaCy's MIT-licensed `en_core_web_sm` 3.8.0, a 12.8 MB hashed GitHub wheel that the Phase 9 dependency gate already provides for, used to generate adjective–noun candidates. YAKE (it ships the AGPL v3), pke (GPL-3.0), textacy (no Python 3.13 Windows wheel for a dependency), summa, gensim 4.x and KeyBERT are all ruled out. On accuracy, published systems with about 15 gold terms per document reach recall@10 of only about 10–22%, even with stemmed matching. AC-08.3 needs about 72% precision@10, where the best SemEval-2010 system reached 32%, and even a perfect top 10 caps the mean at 0.695. A realistic result is roughly 0.20–0.40 (my estimate, not a measurement). A trustworthy verdict needs about 30 fresh documents split before labeling, blind labels under frozen guidelines, a test set scored once with document-level intervals, and a human second annotator on a subset. The result should be reported as agreement with the Fable 5.1 annotator, not as accuracy.

## RAKE loses because it sums scores and filters runs, never splits them

The measured failure is about length, not ranking. In the latest run the configured RAKE variant put **0 of 72 gold terms in the top 10 (Wilson 95% 0.000–0.051)**, and the best variant put in 1. The lenient "one contains the other" diagnostic sat at 0.21–0.24, because the top-10 lists are full of near-misses such as "gin operator classes included" next to the gold terms "gin" and "operator classes" ([run report](../reports/evaluation/enrichment_58c67164e8e84b708c80e6df4721518f.md)). The gold is unusually short. Of 72 terms, **36 are one word, 35 are two words and 1 is three words (mean 1.51)**, although the guidelines allowed up to 4 words ([gold](../datasets/nlp/keyphrases_gold.jsonl); [guidelines](../datasets/nlp/GUIDELINES.md)). Standard benchmark gold averages **2.03–3.00 words, with only 13.5–26.9% single words** ([PromptRank](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.04490)).

The mechanism is visible in rake-nltk's source. A candidate is a maximal run of tokens that are neither stop words nor punctuation, and its score is the sum of each word's degree/frequency. `max_length` only applies the filter `min_length <= len(x) <= max_length`. The docstring's own example shows that the range [1, 1] drops "red apples" rather than yielding "red" and "apples" ([rake-nltk rake.py](https://github.com/csurfer/rake-nltk/blob/master/rake_nltk/rake.py)). TechDocQA already passes `max_length=keyphrase_max_words`, with a default of 4 ([keyphrases.py](../src/techdocqa/enrichment/keyphrases.py)). Lowering it to 2 would keep only runs that happen to stand alone between stop words. A term like "consumer group" inside "kafka consumer group coordinator" would vanish from both the candidate list and the co-occurrence graph.

The literature names this failure. Hasan and Ng's error analysis attributes **28–37% of errors to overgeneration**, which they trace to systems that "sum the scores of a candidate's component words". It attributes another **8–12% to redundancy** ([Hasan & Ng 2014](https://aclanthology.org/P14-1119/)). The project shows both. In the word_degree variant, 8 of K3's top 10 contain "spark", and K4's list spends slots on "gin operator classes" and "gin operator class" ([run report](../reports/evaluation/enrichment_58c67164e8e84b708c80e6df4721518f.md)). PromptRank's length exponent points the same way: the best α is smaller where gold phrases are shorter, 0.2 on NUS (mean 2.03 words) against 1 on Inspec (2.31) ([PromptRank](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.04490)).

The fix therefore has four parts. First, list every 1–2-gram inside each run. Second, score with a function that does not grow with length: a mean or maximum of word scores, or n-gram frequency with a C-value-style discount for occurrences nested in longer strings. The original C-value's log2(length) factor is zero for single words, so it needs the unigram-capable variant ([Frantzi et al. 2000](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s007999900023); [Lossio-Ventura et al. 2013](https://simbig.org/lossio-ventura/LBM2013.pdf)). Third, deduplicate after plural folding. Fourth, exclude the bare technology name, which the guidelines forbid as a key term. One asset is already in place: the project's tokenizer keeps technical tokens whole ([keyphrases.py](../src/techdocqa/enrichment/keyphrases.py)). Any new generator must reuse it, because rake-nltk's default `wordpunct_tokenize` splits `spark.local.dir` into three words ([rake.py](https://github.com/csurfer/rake-nltk/blob/master/rake_nltk/rake.py)), and spaCy's infix rules split "B-tree" at the hyphen ([spaCy punctuation.py](https://github.com/explosion/spaCy/blob/master/spacy/lang/punctuation.py)).

## Four licence-clean routes remain, and the best needs no new package

Checked against the constraints (licence of the shipped file, not just the metadata; Windows wheels for Python 3.13; no torch; offline at run time; maintenance), only four routes survive.

| Route | Licence | What it adds for 1–2-word terms | Install and offline status | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Project code over the existing tokenizer and rake-nltk word statistics | rake-nltk MIT; numpy already locked | Sub-n-gram listing, length-neutral scores, nested-term discount, plural folding | Nothing new ([lock](../requirements.lock.txt)) | **First choice** |
| spaCy `en_core_web_sm` 3.8.0 (Matcher POS patterns, or noun_chunks) | MIT; OntoNotes 5 training data "licensed by Explosion" | `<ADJ>*<NOUN>+` candidates capped at 2 tokens; noun_chunks needs the parser | 12.8 MB py3 wheel, GitHub-hosted (not on PyPI); pinned to spaCy `>=3.8.0,<3.9.0` | **Second choice** |
| scikit-learn 1.9.1 `CountVectorizer`/`TfidfVectorizer` | BSD-3-Clause | `ngram_range=(1,2)`, corpus IDF | cp313 Windows wheels; not installed today; default `token_pattern` splits dotted and hyphenated names | Optional; little gain over own TF-IDF |
| NLTK perceptron tagger + `RegexpParser` | Tagger data MIT | POS chunking without spaCy | `averaged_perceptron_tagger_eng`, 1.5 MB, sha256 in the nltk_data index | Fallback |

Sources for the table: [spaCy model release](https://github.com/explosion/spacy-models/releases/tag/en_core_web_sm-3.8.0), [model meta](https://github.com/explosion/spacy-models/blob/master/meta/en_core_web_sm-3.8.0.json), [PyPI scikit-learn](https://pypi.org/project/scikit-learn/), [CountVectorizer docs](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.feature_extraction.text.CountVectorizer.html), [nltk_data index](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nltk/nltk_data/gh-pages/index.xml).

The GitHub-hosted model wheel is not the obstacle it first looks like. The Phase 9 dependency gate already provides for "the model as a hashed URL". It installs in a scratch environment, confirms the licence files, checks determinism over 3 runs, blocks the network and reinstalls offline with `--require-hashes` ([plan](../docs/PHASES.md#phase-9-plan); [decisions](../docs/DECISIONS.md)). Two caveats remain. The licence reviewer should confirm that Explosion's commercial OntoNotes licence covers MIT redistribution of the weights ([model meta](https://github.com/explosion/spacy-models/blob/master/meta/en_core_web_sm-3.8.0.json)). And no source guarantees bit-identical CPU inference across machines, so determinism tests should compare top-10 lists rather than raw scores. pytextrank 3.3.0 (MIT) would ride on this model. It cannot run on the current `blank("en")` + sentencizer pipeline, because `noun_chunks` raises spaCy's E029 `ValueError` without a parse, while pytextrank only catches `NotImplementedError`. It has also had no release since February 2024 ([pytextrank base.py](https://github.com/DerwenAI/pytextrank/blob/main/pytextrank/base.py); [spaCy syntax_iterators.py](https://github.com/explosion/spaCy/blob/master/spacy/lang/en/syntax_iterators.py)).

The rejections are firm. **YAKE 0.7.3 (February 2026) still ships the GNU Affero GPL v3** under metadata that says LGPLv3, which is why the project's gate already failed it ([PyPI yake](https://pypi.org/project/yake/0.7.3/)). pke is GPL-3.0 and not on PyPI. Its setup.py imports `distutils`, which Python 3.12 removed, and it depends on the GPL-licensed unidecode ([pke](https://github.com/boudinfl/pke); [PEP 632](https://peps.python.org/pep-0632/)). textacy is Apache-2.0 but unmaintained since April 2023, and its hard dependency floret has Windows wheels only up to cp312 ([PyPI floret](https://pypi.org/project/floret/#files)). summa is an sdist with no licence file and no commit since 2019 ([PyPI summa](https://pypi.org/project/summa/#files)). gensim removed its keyword module in 4.0 and is LGPL-2.1-only, not the approved LGPLv3 ([gensim 4.0 release](https://github.com/piskvorky/gensim/releases/tag/4.0.0beta)). multi-rake filters by word count exactly as rake-nltk does ([algorithm.py](https://github.com/vgrabovets/multi_rake/blob/master/multi_rake/algorithm.py)). KeyBERT hard-requires sentence-transformers, and so torch ([PyPI keybert](https://pypi.org/project/keybert/)). An EmbedRank loop over the existing Ollama `nomic-embed-text` service avoids torch through KeyBERT's `BaseEmbedder` ([KeyBERT embeddings guide](https://maartengr.github.io/KeyBERT/guides/embeddings.html)), but it is still embedding-based extraction, so it needs a contract amendment. Open Ollama issues also report all-zero vectors under load and silent truncation ([#17878](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/17878); [#14259](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/14259)).

Latency favours the first route. Keyphrase extraction currently takes **3.4–50.6 ms per document** in the latest run, and 72 ms for the 85,823-character Kafka design page in the final Phase 9 report ([run report](../reports/evaluation/enrichment_58c67164e8e84b708c80e6df4721518f.md); [status](../docs/PHASES.md#phase-9-status)). n-gram counting adds little to that. spaCy's tagger and parser cost on CPU was not found in any primary source, so it must be measured at the gate. Excluding the parser and NER for Matcher-only use cuts it. The literature also suggests effort is better spent on candidates than on rankers. Reviews repeatedly find that phrase formation and post-processing strongly affect results and that TF-IDF is a strong baseline ([Papagiannopoulou & Tsoumakas](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1905.05044)).

## AC-08.3 asks for 72% precision@10, more than double the best published

The target's arithmetic is unforgiving. With 15, 14, 14, 14 and 15 gold terms, a perfect top 10 scores 10/G per document, so **the mean recall@10 ceiling is 0.695**. Reaching 0.50 needs about **36 hits in 50 slots, a precision@10 near 0.72** (own computation from the [gold](../datasets/nlp/keyphrases_gold.jsonl)). The closest published setting is SemEval-2010: full technical papers with about 14.8 gold terms each. There the best of 19 systems, a supervised one, reached **precision@10 of 32.0% and recall@10 of 21.8%**, and the TF-IDF baseline reached recall@10 of 12.1%, all under Porter-stemmed matching ([Kim et al. 2010](https://aclanthology.org/S10-1004/)). Re-run on a common protocol, classical methods score F1@10 of 11–13 on SemEval-2010 (TextRank 11.27, YAKE 11.01, MultipartiteRank 12.91), EmbedRank 16.35 and T5-based PromptRank 20.66 ([PromptRank](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.04490)). A zero-shot Llama3-8B reaches 22.46 ([Kang & Shin 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.248/)). At G ≈ 15, those F1 figures convert to recall@10 of roughly **9–19%**. Cross-paper numbers are unstable: YAKE's Inspec F1@5 is 18.08 in one re-run and 8.02 in another ([Song et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.15156v2)). Treat these as a band, not a point.

TechDocQA's matching is also stricter than every benchmark protocol. It applies NFKC, casefolding and whitespace collapse but no stemming, while SemEval-2010, Meng et al. and Kang & Shin all stem before matching ([Meng et al. 2017](https://aclanthology.org/P17-1054/)). That matters because the gold mixes plurals and singulars, as in "tasks" and "worker", or "topic" and "producers".

Some features of this project pull the other way. The guidelines require terms copied as they appear in the text, "the form used most often", and no bare technology name ([guidelines](../datasets/nlp/GUIDELINES.md)). That removes the 15–19% absent-gold ceiling seen in SemEval-2010 ([Kim et al. 2010](https://aclanthology.org/S10-1004/)), and it rewards an extractor that outputs each term's most frequent surface form. The five documents are short, **4,971–14,148 characters** ([run report](../reports/evaluation/enrichment_58c67164e8e84b708c80e6df4721518f.md)), closer to news length than to full papers, and "F-scores decrease as document length increases" ([Hasan & Ng 2014](https://aclanthology.org/P14-1119/)). Many gold terms are distinctive identifiers ("gin", "brin", "psql", "createdb"). Weighing both sides, my estimate is a mean of **roughly 0.20–0.40** for a well-built short-candidate extractor on a fresh set. A tightly focused page like K4 (index types) could approach 0.5 on its own. No source measures this genre, so the estimate needs testing, not trust.

The more productive lever is the metric itself. The guidelines already ask for terms "most important first", so scoring against each document's first 10 ranked gold terms removes the 10/G ceiling without relabeling. R-precision does the same job in established form ([Hasan & Ng 2014](https://aclanthology.org/P14-1119/)). Plural folding is a defensible secondary rule: judges accepted plural variants 96% of the time, against only 44% for the "extracted is part of gold" half of the current lenient diagnostic ([Zesch & Gurevych 2009](https://aclanthology.org/R09-1086/)). Any such amendment must be recorded before the test set is drawn. Changing the metric after seeing results is leakage too.

## The five used documents become the dev set; a fresh test set is scored once

K1–K5 are no longer independent. Their gold lists and the extractor's top-10 lists sit side by side in the run report, so every design choice (a 2-word cap, technology-name exclusion, stop-list edits, a title or heading boost) is now informed by them. They should become development data only. The 15 other corpus documents are too few for a test set, and their `enrich` output may already have been looked at. A clean test set should come from new pages of the four products' public documentation, stratified by technology, format (HTML, Markdown, PDF) and length, with duplicate pages from other versions or formats excluded. PDFs deserve their own stratum because of headers, footers and line-break hyphenation, the same problem SemEval-2010 documented ([Kim et al. 2010](https://aclanthology.org/S10-1004/)).

Size follows from the intervals. Pooled over terms, the Wilson 95% half-width at p = 0.5 is ±0.113 for 72 terms and ±0.069 for 200. But AC-08.3 is a mean over documents, so the honest interval is document-level, and it is wider. Assuming a document-to-document SD of 0.15–0.20, which is not measured, the t-interval half-width is:

| Test documents | SD 0.15 | SD 0.20 |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | ±0.186 | ±0.248 |
| 10 | ±0.107 | ±0.143 |
| 20 | ±0.070 | ±0.094 |
| 30 | ±0.056 | ±0.075 |

(Own computation.) About 20 test documents is the minimum that separates a pass at 0.50 from a result near 0.40. Underpowered comparisons are common in NLP, and power analysis should come first ([Card et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.745/)). A practical design is about 30 fresh documents, with a random split frozen before annotation: about 10 for development and about 20 for test.

The procedure should leave no gap for tuning to leak through. Freeze and hash the guidelines first, amended if the owner chooses to fix exactly 10 ranked terms per document. The annotator labels from document text alone, which the current guidelines already require, and its identity is recorded: model, version, prompt hash and date. Use the existing freeze mechanism, which records gold SHA-256 hashes in every run. Run an automatic presence check and report recall over present terms as primary. Record the extractor's configuration ID and code hash before the test labels are opened. If an AI coding assistant writes the extractor, keep the test gold files out of its context. If TF-IDF is used, fix the document-frequency corpus before the test. Then score the test set once, compare it with current RAKE on the same documents using a paired document bootstrap, and report the document-level lower bound beside the point estimate, as the project already does with "met at point estimate only". A second round needs a new test set, not a rerun.

## Gold from Fable 5.1 measures agreement with one model's style

The owner designated Fable 5.1, an AI model, to label the key terms and approved this as a recorded deviation. The scores therefore "measure agreement with an AI annotator, not human ground truth" ([review log](../datasets/nlp/review_log.md)). Its style is distinctive: **50% single words and 1.4% of three or more words, against 20.8% and 31.9% in human benchmark gold**. LLM keyphrase length also swings with prompt wording: GPT-4o produced 8.6% single words when asked for "keyphrases" and 23.8% when asked for "keywords" ([Kang & Shin 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.248/)). "Shorter candidates" is therefore partly an optimisation toward this annotator. A fresh set labeled by the same model with the same prompt controls document leakage, but not overfitting to its style.

Human agreement offers the right yardstick. In SemEval-2010, readers' keyphrases scored only **F 33.6% against the authors' own** ([Kim et al. 2010](https://aclanthology.org/S10-1004/)). In a 357-person panel, more than half of all keyphrases were chosen by a single annotator, and 17% of one annotator's "negatives" were keyphrases to someone else ([Sterckx et al. 2016](https://aclanthology.org/D16-1198/)). Using one LLM's output as silver labels estimated extraction error less accurately than ensemble disagreement, when both were checked against human labels ([Du et al. 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.gem-1.5/)). The remedy is cheap. One or two people independently label 5–10 test documents, blind to the AI lists and to extractor output. Human-vs-AI agreement is then reported in the same top-10 metric, next to extractor-vs-AI agreement. If two or more humans take part, the "alternative annotator test" is a formal way to decide whether the AI annotator can stand in for them ([Calderon et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10970)). Pooling human and AI lists, as SemEval-2010's combined gold did, also raises attainable recall honestly ([Kim et al. 2010](https://aclanthology.org/S10-1004/)).

## Conclusion

The real decision in AC-08.3 is the metric, not the algorithm. Even a perfect extractor tops out at 0.695 under the current definition, and published methods reach about a third of that. Shortening candidates will turn many of today's lenient near-misses into exact hits, but no licence-clean library closes the remaining gap. The owner should therefore settle the metric (ranked top-10 gold or R-precision, and plural folding) before the fresh set is drawn. The cheapest high-value experiment on the dev set is an oracle check: how many gold terms appear anywhere among the 1–2-gram candidates. That separates candidate failures from ranking failures before any library is gated in.

The second shift concerns what a pass would mean. Against an owner-approved AI annotator whose style favours single words, a high score proves fit to that annotator's style, not usefulness to readers. The human-labeled subset is what turns the result into evidence. If extractor-vs-AI agreement approaches human-vs-AI agreement, the extractor is about as good as this gold can show, whatever the 50% line says.
