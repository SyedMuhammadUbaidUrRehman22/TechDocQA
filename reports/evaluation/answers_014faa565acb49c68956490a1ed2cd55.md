## full-kafka-03

**Question:** If every in-sync replica of a partition fails, how does Kafka choose the next leader by default, and what does that trade away?

Status `answered`, 5 of 5 chunks used, 13193 ms.

> By default, when every in-sync replica of a partition fails, Kafka chooses the first replica (not necessarily in the ISR) that comes back to life as the leader [C4]. This trade-off is between availability and consistency. Waiting for replicas in the ISR ensures availability but risks permanent downtime if those replicas fail. Allowing a non-in-sync replica to become leader ensures consistency but may result in data loss if that replica fails [C4].

Warnings: 

- [C4] Design | Apache Kafka > Design > Replication > Unclean leader election: What if they all die? (https://kafka.apache.org/43/design/design/) `chk_5552c0c7d131`

> Window functions differ from ordinary aggregates like `avg()` in that they do not group rows into a single output row. Instead, they retain the separate identities of each row.
